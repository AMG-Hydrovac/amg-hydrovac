Test Holes Before You Dig

Daylighting & Utility Potholing

Daylighting, or utility potholing, is a hydrovac test hole that exposes a buried line so you can see its depth, size and material before a drill head or bucket goes near it. AMG Hydrovac digs test holes across Toronto and the GTA for the crews that have to work beside what is buried. Each hole is measured, photographed and handed over as a record you can design and dig from.

Locates Mark. Potholing Proves.

An Ontario One Call locate puts paint on the surface. It tells you roughly where a line runs, not how deep it sits, what it is made of, or whether it was installed where the drawing says. In older GTA neighbourhoods the as-built and the real line do not always agree.

A test hole settles it. With the ticket confirmed and any private locates in, we daylight the mark so your crew works from the exposed line, not the paint.

  • Depth of cover to the top of the utility, measured from grade
  • Outside diameter and material of the pipe, duct or cable
  • Horizontal offset between the locate mark and the actual line

Before the Drill Head or the Bucket Goes In

Pressurized water loosens the soil and the vacuum pulls the slurry into the 9 m³ (12 cu yd) debris tank. Nothing with teeth or an edge touches the ground, so the gas main, duct bank or fibre bundle is exposed intact. In frozen ground we run heated water and keep digging.

Who orders them: HDD crews proving crossings along a bore path, general contractors digging beside live services, utilities verifying their own plant before a tie-in, engineering and survey firms collecting subsurface utility data for design, and telecom builders threading conduit through a full corridor.

Working in Congested Corridors

A downtown Toronto boulevard can carry gas, water, hydro, telecom and a duct bank within a couple of metres of each other. Hydrovac suits that ground because the hole stays the size you need and the lines you expose are not disturbed.

Remote hose reaches up to 90 m (300 ft) from the truck when it cannot park beside the dig, so the unit can sit in a lane closure while the hole goes in across the sidewalk. The boom reaches 8 m (26 ft), or 12.5 m (41 ft) extended, on a 342° turret, and a removable deflector plate drops the height under overhead lines. The operator is Book 7 trained for the live road allowance.

How Many Holes in a Day

Everyone asks for a holes-per-day number and the honest answer is that it depends. Soil (GTA clay and compacted fill dig slower than sand), target depth, how far the truck sits from each hole, whether the holes are clustered or strung along a kilometre of road, and the distance to refill water and offload all move it. Frost adds time in winter.

Send the locate sheet or drawing with expected depths and spacing and we give you a realistic production estimate before you commit, not a number that looks good on paper.

The Record and the Restoration

Every hole gets a photo-and-depth record: the exposed utility with a tape in frame, depth from grade to top of pipe, size, material and offset from the locate mark or your reference point. It goes to you with the daily report, in a form your designer or surveyor can carry onto the drawing.

The spoil leaves in the tank as slurry and goes to a licensed receiving facility under our MECP EASR registration; disposal is its own line on the quote. Tell us at booking how you want the hole closed: left open and protected for your crew to tie in, or backfilled and compacted in lifts with the material you specify and left ready for surface restoration.
